# CONTRACT.md — Dial

Dial's observable product requirements. Each clause has a stable ID. `pnpm run verify` runs the product checks; the manifest validator reports structural integrity only at the current maturity, not full behavioral acceptance. Security, privacy and operational requirements are in `BASELINE.md`; explicit supersessions are named below. Verification evidence and uncovered journeys are recorded in HANDOVER.md. Capacity and rate limits are declared in seed.json.

## Scope

Dial is a scheduler for one business or one independent host. Operators publish a host profile and one or more event types, optionally connect the host's calendars, and define when bookings may occur. An invitee books without an account, in their own timezone, on a time rechecked against the host's saved availability and persisted bookings at confirmation, plus live busy data when a calendar is connected; the booking then carries an optional managed calendar event, a location, email that says what actually happened, and a private link to reschedule or cancel. An invitee who finds nothing suitable can ask the host for another window.

## Authorisation

- **AUTH-001 — Public authority.** Supersedes `BASE-ACCESS-004` for the paths listed in `seed.json → deploy.publicPaths` and for no others. Those paths authorise the caller by the capability material named in `AUTH-002` to `AUTH-006` instead of an operator session, each capability grants exactly the one action or record it names, and a request carrying no valid capability material changes nothing. Every other mutation requires a valid operator session and a server-side ownership check.
- **AUTH-002 — Booking nonce.** Reaching the details step for a selected slot, and reaching the review step of an alternative request, each issue a single-use nonce of at least 256 bits from a cryptographically secure random source, bound to the event type and to either the selected slot or the composed alternative request, and expiring 30 minutes after issue. The nonce is not bound to a browser: possession of an unexpired, unconsumed nonce is what authorises the confirmation. A confirmation whose nonce is missing, unknown, expired, or bound to different values creates nothing and answers per `PUBLIC-003`; a nonce that has already been consumed is answered by `BOOK-003`. After an alternative request is submitted, that same consumed nonce grants read-only access to its acknowledgement delivery state, including after nonce expiry. This receipt grants no additional read or mutation beyond the existing confirmation-replay behavior of `BOOK-003` and `REQUEST-002`, which remains unchanged. The receipt returns only queued, sent, or failed, remains bound to the public event and original request, and becomes unavailable when either is unavailable or erased.
- **AUTH-003 — Booking access.** A booking is readable and changeable from a public surface only through the manage token defined in `BOOK-006`. A booking ID, an invitee email address, or any other guessable identifier grants access to no booking data.
- **AUTH-004 — Calendar authorisation callback.** The calendar OAuth callback accepts only signed state that is bound to the operator session that started the connection and was issued less than 10 minutes earlier. State that is missing, unsigned, mismatched, replayed, or expired stores no credential, connects no account, and returns 400.
- **AUTH-005 — Provider callbacks.** A calendar or mail provider callback is accepted only when the provider's own authentication passes — its registered channel identity or its request signature — together with the secret configured for that provider. A callback that fails the check returns 401 and changes nothing.

- **AUTH-006 — First-owner admission.** On an empty native authentication store, a local CLI install accepts its setup token through the native Base bootstrap route. When managed owner-claim bindings are present, that route is closed and only a short-lived ES256 signed claim may admit the first owner. The issuer, application origin, installation identity, public verification keys and owner email come from trusted deployment configuration. Invalid claims write nothing. Successful admission atomically records a permanent consumed claim, creates the native owner and product identity, and issues a native session; concurrent losers roll back, and subsequent admission is refused after restart or removal of the original owner. Existing operator sessions, sign-in and equal-operator management retain their native protocol.

## Operators

- **OPER-001 — Equal operators.** Every operator can see and act on the same host profile, event types, availability, calendar connections, bookings, and requests. There are no roles.
- **OPER-002 — Operator management.** Operators invite and remove other operators in Settings. Removal revokes access and erases the removed native identity, sessions, link tokens, external identity bindings, local mail and email-scoped rate records. Unrelated authored records and the shared calendar remain, with an anonymous author reference. A new invitation creates a new identity and cannot revive previous capabilities. Base remains the access authority and protects the last owner. Identity overlap with invitee records requires explicitly erasing those records through the aggregate email confirmation or their detail pages before operator removal. Self-removal and removal of the protected setup/bootstrap owner return an explicit hold; an owner-transfer workflow is required before that identity can be erased. A shared domain policy retains its original creator ID as an inactive anonymous native reference whose authority cannot be reactivated. This replaces the earlier access-only removal and setup self-removal policy. *Policy: `operator.management.v1`; default: the invitation and removal behavior specified in this clause, implemented directly in product services; authored replacement dispatch is not implemented.*
- **OPER-003 — Booking workspace.** Operators can list bookings filtered by the lifecycle states of `BOOK-004`, and independently by whether the start instant is in the future or the past, by event type, by date range, and by whether any external delivery has failed. Case-insensitive substring search over invitee name and email address returns within 500ms. Lists are sorted by start instant.
- **OPER-004 — Booking record.** A booking record shows the event type, every invitee answer and custom-field value, the start and end in both the host's and the invitee's IANA zones, the location or meeting link, the history from `BOOK-010`, the separate state of the calendar event, the meeting link, and each email, and every recorded failure with its `CAL-005` or `NOTIFY-005` reason.

- **EDITOR-001 — Owner editor.** When configured, the native operator app mounts the Base editor only after an active native owner session authorizes a private, non-cached grant lasting at most 30 seconds. Session or grant expiry, revocation, refused refresh and logout remove editor authority. The app permits framing only by itself and the exact trusted management origin, retaining unrelated security directives. All Dial operators have the same native owner capability under OPER-001. Sessionless callers receive no editor configuration.
- **OPER-005 — Durable first run.** New operators authenticate through the native sign-in flow and arrive at private `/setup`. Until the installation records completion, every `/app` document and hydrated app navigation redirects to `/setup` before workspace content; private APIs remain session-protected and callable by the setup editors. Setup provides the same real profile, connection, event and availability editors. A same-origin authenticated finish command requires a currently valid host profile and a published event with a persisted copy report for its current canonical booking URL, revalidating EVENT-006 readiness and the profile/event/report snapshot. **Copy booking link** persists that report after its clipboard write succeeds; when clipboard access is denied it shows the booking link in a selectable read-only field, and **I have shared it** persists the same report through the same authenticated, same-origin route. Request-only events require alternative requests and a successful mail check, including the explicit local development outbox; they require no Google Calendar or availability window. Automatic booking requires saved availability and verified mail; a calendar is optional for fixed locations, while connected calendars and Google Meet retain their provider checks. Completion records a server timestamp once. Completed `/setup` visits go to `/app`; aging connection health, later edits or deletion never reopen first run. Owner reinvitation, setup reruns and whole-install export/restore preserve the marker. The forward migration marks already-existing setup identities with their creation time solely for upgrade compatibility, without asserting historical checklist completion. Owners claimed after migration start unfinished. Reset removes local completion with local owner data; the next fresh claim must complete setup again.

## Profile and event types

- **PROFILE-001 — Host profile.** Operators edit the host's name, title, biography, avatar, IANA timezone, focus areas, and social-proof entries, within the counts and lengths in `seed.json → limits`. A social-proof entry is a quotation and an attribution typed by an operator: Dial collects no invitee feedback and publishes no rating, score, or count it did not receive from an operator. There is exactly one host timezone per deployment and it is the zone every other clause means by "the host's zone". The in-app preview renders the same content as the public page.
- **PROFILE-002 — Public profile.** The host profile is served at `/book/<handle>`, where the handle is set by an operator. It shows the configured host content and every published, listed event type, and never a draft, unlisted, or disabled one. An operator can change the handle; the previous handle then answers per `PUBLIC-005`.
- **PROFILE-003 — Avatar asset.** Supersedes `BASE-INPUT-003` for the `/media/` prefix only. The avatar is an upload within the size and type limits in `seed.json → limits`, stored in R2 and served under `/media/` with its declared content type and inline rather than with a download disposition, because it renders as an image on the public profile and event pages. That prefix is the public delivery channel the baseline requires a product contract to name; every other upload rule still applies, and no other upload in this product is publicly readable.
- **EVENT-001 — Event type lifecycle.** Operators create, edit, publish, disable, and delete event types. An event type is draft, published, or disabled, and a published one is either listed on the public profile or unlisted and reachable only by its own URL. Each carries a title, description, a single duration of 5 to 480 minutes, colour, location, and a slug unique among event types, and is served at `/book/<handle>/<slug>`. A slug can be edited; the previous slug is not retained and then answers per `PUBLIC-005`, while existing bookings and their manage links are unaffected.
- **EVENT-002 — Scheduling constraints.** Each event type configures its recurring availability, date-specific overrides, slot start increment of 5, 10, 15, 30, or 60 minutes, minimum notice of 0 minutes to 7 days, maximum booking horizon of 1 day to `seed.json → limits.availabilityLookaheadDays`, before and after buffers of 0 to 120 minutes each, and an optional maximum number of bookings per host day, where a host day is one calendar date in the host's timezone. A new event type starts at a 30-minute duration, a 15-minute increment, 4 hours' minimum notice, a 60-day horizon, no buffers, and no host-day limit.
- **EVENT-003 — Invitee questions.** Each event type has ordered booking questions of type text, textarea, single choice, multiple choice, or checkbox, each required or optional, within the question and option counts in `seed.json → limits`. Choice options have stable values and editable labels, and a submitted option not configured on the event type is rejected. A name and a syntactically valid email address are always required. *Policy: `booking.questions.v1`; default: name and email, plus an optional agenda of at most 500 characters, on every new event type.*
- **EVENT-004 — Location.** An event type uses exactly one location: a Google Meet conference provisioned per `CAL-003`, a fixed link the operator supplies, a host phone number the invitee calls, or an in-person address. The public event page shows only the location's label; the link, number, or street address appears only after confirmation — in the receipt, the invitee's email, and the calendar event. Dial never collects an invitee phone number.
- **EVENT-005 — Removing an event type.** Supersedes `BASE-DATA-003` for event types only: disabling or deleting an event type removes its public page and all its future availability but does not cascade to its bookings, which keep their times, manage links, calendar events, history, and their own copy of the event title, duration, and location. Invitee data is erased under `DATA-002`, not by deleting an event type.
- **EVENT-006 — Publication readiness.** An event type can be published only when it has at least one open availability window and when its transactional-mail configuration, any connected destination calendar, and, where its location is provider-generated, its conferencing capability each passed a connection check within the last 24 hours or since they last changed. No connected calendar is required for a fixed link, phone number or in-person address: these events use saved availability minus persisted Dial bookings. Publishing explicitly turns on real bookings, with a notice that no external calendar conflicts are checked when disconnected. Google Meet always requires a healthy conference-capable destination calendar. An unhealthy or incomplete connection is never treated as absence. A connection check is a live read of the connected destination calendar, a credential probe against the mail provider, and, where conferencing is used, a capability check against the conferencing provider; an operator can run it on demand. The operator sees each unmet requirement named. A requirement that becomes unhealthy after publication does not unpublish the event type: the connection state changes under `CAL-006` and public booking behaves under `AVAIL-004`. As an explicit exception, an operator may open a draft or disabled event in request-only mode with a saved valid host profile/timezone, alternative requests enabled and a successful transactional-mail connection check. Request-only publication requires no open availability window, destination calendar or conferencing connection, offers no availability or bookable slots, and rejects booking creation; proposed request windows do not reserve time. Enabling automatic booking uses the normal publication checks above and clears request-only mode only after they pass.

## Public home

- **HOME-001 — Product landing.** An installed deployment's root `/` is the sessionless installed Lobby. Before setup completion is recorded it says the installation is waiting for its owner and offers a **Finish setup** action to `/setup`; after completion it names the deployment, offers a **Sign in** action and credits Dial with a link to its run site. A saved host profile alone never changes that state. The Lobby reads only the setup state, sets no cookie, writes nothing to browser storage, runs no analytics and has one top-level heading. The product landing for Dial itself is the separate run site at `run-dial.com`, built from `src/site` and deployed by the Run site workflow. It presents, in order, a hero with the product's headline, a description, directly beneath it in small text the single line "100% free, open source alternative to Calendly, Acuity Scheduling and SavvyCal" (the three company names in bold, the words of a name joined by a non-breaking space, the line wrapped evenly; repeated in plain text at the start of the page's meta description, with no email- or phone-shaped text and no comparative claim), then a **Run Dial** action to `/run` and a **Read the docs** action to `/docs`; sections for invitees, for the host, the booking essentials, alternative requests and self-hosting; and a closing section repeating both actions. Its header links home, to `/docs` and to the source repository, and its footer repeats those links as plain text links beside a small wordmark and single-line description; neither links an installation route (`/signin`, `/setup`, `/app`, `/auth`, `/book`). The site reads no installation data, sets no cookie, writes nothing to browser storage, runs no analytics or tracking, and loads only same-origin resources, with its typeface self-hosted and every drawing inline and decorative. It has one top-level heading, is readable without script, renders its final state with reduced motion (its only motion is a one-shot hero sequence), and keeps a fixed light palette regardless of the browser's colour scheme. An installation's own public chrome (its `/docs` pages) adds a link to `/signin`, names the deployment-configured owner only when the deployment configures one (a new install configures none), and shows the `Powered by Dial · runeditrun.com` footer credit as a static link controlled by deployment configuration, enabled by default and omitted when disabled.
- **DOCS-001 — Public documentation.** The public paths `/docs` and `/docs/<slug>` serve Dial's first-party documentation: the Markdown files that `docs/site.json` lists as published, in the sections and order it gives, which is the only navigation. The documentation Markdown is rendered to HTML at build time: a Vite plugin renders each page when Vite loads it, in a build and in development, and never in the Worker or the browser, so the emitted Worker and client bundles contain no Markdown or syntax-highlighting code, and each page is its own lazily loaded chunk that the Worker renders per request inside the public site's shared header and footer. A page has its title as its one top-level heading (the header's wordmark is not a heading on a documentation page), a section menu, an on-this-page list, previous and next links, tables in labelled scrollable regions and an Edit-on-GitHub link. Every Markdown file at the repository root and under `docs/` is either published or listed in the manifest as GitHub-only with a reason, and an unlisted file, a broken link or anchor, raw HTML, an image outside `docs/media/` or an absolute build-machine path fails verification or the build. The documentation is served by `GET` only, reads no booking, invitee, host-profile or operator record or session, sets no cookie, runs no analytics or tracking and loads only same-origin resources. An unknown, miscased or nested slug answers the existing `404` page, and `/docs` shares the public admission budget of the other sessionless public paths.

## Public booking surfaces

- **PUBLIC-001 — Event presentation.** A published event page names the host and the event and shows its description, duration, public location label, the invitee's selected timezone, a day selector with each invitee-zone date shown once, up to five suggested times first for the selected day, each chosen by the native ranking of `AVAIL-007` and showing its reason, and every valid slot in the displayed week reachable by selecting its day. Only the selected day's times are listed; the initial selection is the first day with a valid slot. Week-by-week navigation is bounded by today and the booking horizon. The page also shows the change window the `booking.reschedule.v1` and `booking.cancellation.v1` policies currently enforce, and, when the event type enables it, the alternative-request path.
- **PUBLIC-002 — Invitee-first flow.** An invitee views availability and completes a booking without an account and without signing in. Before confirmation the flow asks only for the event type's configured questions, names who receives the answers — the host and, when connected, the calendar provider — and links the privacy notice URL configured for the deployment.
- **PUBLIC-003 — Progress and recovery.** The public flow has distinct choose-time, details, and confirmation states, each at its own URL, and reloading or navigating back preserves a still-valid selection. The selection and its nonce are carried in the URL and in the browser's session storage; the public flow sets no cookie. An expired nonce, or an expired or rotated manage token, returns 410; an unknown or mismatched one returns 404. Both render a page that offers a fresh start and that contains no booking, invitee, or sample data.
- **PUBLIC-004 — Embed.** Operators copy an asynchronous script tag naming a public event slug and `inline` or `modal` mode. Inline mode renders where the tag sits; modal mode opens only from an element whose `data-dial-embed` attribute names that embed. The initial script is under 50KB gzipped, and the embedded flow offers the same booking, reschedule, and cancel actions as the standalone page.
- **PUBLIC-005 — Unavailable pages.** An unknown, draft, disabled, deleted, or renamed profile or event URL returns 404 and reveals nothing about whether a record ever existed there. This governs profile and event URLs only; manage-token responses are governed by `PUBLIC-003`, where a rotated or expired token deliberately returns 410 so the invitee knows to ask for a new link. A published event type with no valid slot inside its horizon still returns 200, says so, and offers the alternative-request path when the event type enables it.
- **PUBLIC-006 — Abuse.** A public request refused by the rate limits of `BASE-ACCESS-003` returns 429 and the flow keeps the invitee's selection and answers so the same person can retry without re-entering them. An operator can block an email address or a domain; a submission from a blocked address is refused with a message that does not say it was blocked, and creates no booking, request, notification, or calendar event.

## Availability

- **AVAIL-001 — Schedule and overrides.** Recurring availability is a set of wall-clock windows per weekday in the host's IANA timezone. A date override replaces the recurring windows for one host date and may open or close that whole date. Editing a schedule or an override changes future availability only; it never alters a confirmed booking.
- **AVAIL-002 — Valid slots.** A slot is offered only when every one of these holds: its start falls on the event type's start increment measured from the start of its open window; the full duration fits inside that one window; the start is within the booking horizon and beyond the minimum notice; its host day is under the event type's booking limit; and it overlaps no booking in the `confirmed` state for this host on any event type and no busy interval from any selected conflict calendar, an external event the provider marks free being ignored. Dial's own managed event, read back from a conflict calendar where Dial last confirmed writing it, is the booking it represents rather than an external busy interval, so a cancelled or moved booking releases its old time under `BOOK-007` and `BOOK-008` without waiting for the provider or its cached busy data. Anything else is not offered.
- **AVAIL-003 — Buffers.** The event type's before and after buffers extend the interval that must be free around a candidate slot, over both confirmed Dial bookings and external busy intervals. A buffer never changes the invitee's start or end time, the duration shown anywhere, or the times on the calendar event and invitation.
- **AVAIL-004 — Freshness and failure.** Availability is recomputed when the event page or a week loads. Changing the invitee's timezone re-renders and re-ranks the same slots and needs no new calendar read, because slot validity does not depend on the invitee's zone. A displayed slot may rest on cached provider busy data no more than 5 minutes old; the final check in `BOOK-001` uses provider data no more than 60 seconds old. With no connected calendar, saved schedule rules and persisted Dial bookings are authoritative and no provider read occurs. This does not relax checks on an unhealthy, incomplete or revoked connection. Every selected conflict calendar is required, and a provider read that does not complete within 5 seconds counts as unreadable. If a required calendar is unreadable at either point, Dial offers and confirms no slot for that host and tells the invitee that booking is temporarily unavailable, rather than offering unverified time.
- **AVAIL-005 — Timezones.** Every invitee-facing time is rendered in the invitee's selected timezone, which starts as the browser's IANA zone when the browser reports one and as the host's zone when it does not. The zone is resolved in the browser and slot instants are fetched in UTC, so no visitor-derived value reaches the server on page load, as `BASE-PUBLIC-001` requires: the invitee's zone is sent only with a request their own action causes — changing the zone, moving week, or selecting a slot. The invitee can change the zone at any point before confirmation. The selected-day group names the invitee's zone once, and every selectable time carries its UTC offset so repeated wall-clock times remain distinct. Other invitee-facing times carry their zone name and UTC offset. In the selected-day picker, host time is shown only when the host's zone differs from the invitee's, including the host date when the local dates differ. The details and confirmation surfaces also show the host's local time, including when the zones match, preserving the receipt's both-zone requirement in `BOOK-005`. A confirmed booking stores the UTC instant and both the host's and the invitee's IANA zone identifiers.
- **AVAIL-006 — Daylight saving time.** A local time that does not exist during a forward transition is never offered. Repeated wall-clock times during a backward transition are shown with distinct UTC offsets, so each offered time maps to exactly one instant. A DST transition between confirmation and the meeting never moves a booking's stored UTC instant, and the host and invitee renderings each follow their own zone's rules.
- **AVAIL-007 — Recommendations and filters.** At most 5 recommended times are shown, each a slot already valid under `AVAIL-002` and each showing the reason it was chosen. The ordering is deterministic: the same inputs always produce the same order. The browser ranks the verified offers in both the host's and invitee's selected zones from the first display. It uses a free-time ordering supplied with the offers, without sending the device zone on page load or disclosing private occupancy intervals; action-triggered server reads use the selected zone under `AVAIL-005`. An invitee-facing filter only narrows or reorders slots already valid under `AVAIL-002` and can never surface one that is not. *Policy: `availability.recommendation.v1`; default: slots fully inside 09:00–17:00 in both the host's and the invitee's selected zone first, then the larger sum of contiguous free minutes immediately before and after the slot, then the earlier UTC start.*
- **AVAIL-008 — Response time.** Loading one week of availability for one event type returns within 2 seconds.
- **AVAIL-009 — Operator diagnostics.** For any time an operator evaluates, Dial names which rule excluded it — schedule, date override, start increment, minimum notice, horizon, buffer, host-day limit, an existing Dial booking, or a conflict-calendar busy interval — without disclosing external event detail that `CAL-002` does not retain.

## Booking lifecycle

- **BOOK-001 — Final validation.** Confirming a booking revalidates every answer against the event type's current questions and rechecks the selected slot under `AVAIL-002` against persisted Dial bookings and, when connected, a fresh conflict-calendar read. Any failure creates no booking, no calendar event, and no email, and names the reason: an invalid or missing answer returns 400, and a slot that is no longer valid returns 409.
- **BOOK-002 — Atomic claim.** Every slot admits at most one booking in the `confirmed` state. Claiming the slot and recording its applicable calendar, meeting, and notification commands happen in one transaction that either commits entirely or leaves nothing behind. Of any number of concurrent confirmations for the same slot exactly one commits; every other returns 409 with a `slot_taken` code and is returned to the choose-time step with availability recomputed.
- **BOOK-003 — Idempotent confirmation.** Replaying a confirmation with an already-consumed nonce and an identical payload returns the stored result of the original booking and creates no second booking, calendar event, meeting link, or email. Replaying it with a different payload returns 409 and creates nothing.
- **BOOK-004 — States and occurrences.** A booking is confirmed, cancelled, or rescheduled, and has one or more occurrences of which exactly one is current: a reschedule closes the current occurrence and opens a new one under the same booking ID and iCalendar UID. The calendar event, the meeting link, and each email carry their own pending, succeeded, or failed state; none of them changes the booking's state, and no external failure silently cancels or moves a booking.
- **BOOK-005 — Confirmation receipt.** Within 2 seconds of a successful claim the invitee is shown the event, the start and end in their own zone and in the host's, the duration, the host, the location or its pending state, the calendar file of `CAL-009`, and the manage link of `BOOK-006`. The join control is enabled only when a provisioned joining URL is stored on the booking; while `CAL-003` is still pending it is disabled and labelled as pending.
- **BOOK-006 — Manage link.** Every confirmed booking has a manage link whose token carries at least 256 bits from a cryptographically secure random source and grants view and change access to that one booking, without creating an invitee account. The token is transmitted only to the invitee, in their own email and on their confirmation page; operator surfaces show the booking but never the token, offering an action that copies or resends the link instead. A reschedule keeps the token, which always resolves to the booking's current occurrence. The token expires 90 days after the booking's start instant, and an operator can rotate it, which invalidates the previous token immediately. A request with an expired, rotated, or unknown token answers per `PUBLIC-003` and discloses no booking data.
- **BOOK-007 — Invitee rescheduling.** The holder of a booking's manage link can move it to another slot on the same event type. The new slot is validated under `BOOK-001` with only this booking's own occurrence excluded from internal conflicts, then claimed under `BOOK-002` before the old slot is released; if any step fails, the original booking is unchanged and still confirmed. *Policy: `booking.reschedule.v1`; default: any number of reschedules, allowed until the booking's start.*
- **BOOK-008 — Invitee cancellation.** The holder of a booking's manage link can cancel it with an optional reason. Cancellation cannot be reversed, releases the slot and its buffers within 5 seconds and before the next availability computation, and repeating it returns the same result without sending a second notification. *Policy: `booking.cancellation.v1`; default: allowed until the booking's start.*
- **BOOK-009 — Operator changes.** An operator can reschedule or cancel any confirmed booking under `BOOK-002`, `BOOK-007`, `BOOK-008`, `BOOK-010`, `CAL-003`, and `NOTIFY-004`. The reschedule and cancellation policies constrain invitees only: an operator may also act on a booking that has already started, and may reschedule onto a time outside published availability after confirming that override explicitly, but never onto a time that overlaps another confirmed booking. An imported booking that a catch-up lists as cancelled at the source may instead be released under `DATA-003`.
- **BOOK-010 — History.** Rescheduling records the original occurrence as rescheduled and links it to its replacement. Every creation, cancellation, and reschedule records who acted — the manage-link holder, the named operator, or the calendar provider under `CAL-008` — the time, the previous and new start, and any reason given.
- **BOOK-011 — Attendance.** After a booking has ended an operator can mark it attended or a no-show and add a note. The mark records the operator and the time, appears in the booking record and the export, changes no lifecycle state and no calendar event, and sends the invitee nothing.
- **BOOK-012 — Operator-created booking.** An operator can create a booking, for an invitee whose name and email address they enter, on any slot valid under `AVAIL-002`, optionally overriding the minimum notice and the horizon but never the conflict and buffer checks. The invitee then receives the ordinary `NOTIFY-001` confirmation and manage link, and `BOOK-010` records the operator as the actor.

## Calendars and meetings

- **CAL-001 — Calendar connection.** An operator connects the host's Google Calendar account through OAuth under `AUTH-004`, selects up to `seed.json → limits.maxConflictCalendars` calendars whose busy intervals block availability, and selects exactly one calendar to receive Dial's bookings. There is exactly one connection; it belongs to the deployment rather than to the operator who authorised it, reconnecting replaces it and keeps the destination selection, and an operator can disconnect. Calendar connection is optional for fixed locations. After an explicit disconnect, sync stops and the connection shows as disconnected. Fixed-location published event types keep offering times from saved availability minus existing bookings; Google Meet offers no slots until its provider is restored. Existing managed bookings retain their calendar obligation and cannot be rescheduled while disconnected; their history is unchanged. The implementation is replaceable through `calendar.provider.v2`.
- **CAL-002 — Busy privacy.** A calendar read retains only the identifiers, time bounds, transparency, and deletion state needed for conflict detection and sync. Titles, descriptions, attendees, and locations from conflict calendars are never stored, and so are never shown to an invitee or to an operator.
- **CAL-003 — Managed event.** A booking made without a connected calendar durably records calendar delivery as not required, schedules no calendar commands on creation, reschedule, cancellation or erasure, and never claims provider success. Its receipt, mail and `.ics` remain available. Connecting later does not retroactively enqueue these bookings; a later reschedule checked against that connection can create its managed event. For a claim using a connected calendar, within 60 seconds the destination calendar holds one event for the booking carrying the event title, the booking's start and end with explicit timezone data, a stable iCalendar UID, the configured location or a Google Meet conference provisioned for this booking, and the invitee as an attendee so the provider's own invitation and updates also reach them — or the booking's calendar state is pending and `CAL-005` applies. An unsuccessful or ambiguous result is handled under `CAL-004` and `CAL-005`.
- **CAL-004 — Idempotent side effects.** Every calendar and meeting command carries the booking and occurrence IDs as its provider idempotency key. After a timeout, a lost response, or any other ambiguous result, the next attempt looks the object up by that key and updates it instead of creating a second one.
- **CAL-005 — Provider failure.** A transient calendar or meeting failure is retried up to 5 times over 1 hour. Until it succeeds the operator and the invitee receipt show the side effect as pending; after the retries are exhausted the operator sees a typed sanitised error code, safe remediation, and a retry action, while the invitee sees only that calendar setup is incomplete and that they should contact the host. The booking stays confirmed, and raw provider errors are neither persisted nor rendered.
- **CAL-006 — Connection health.** Revoked credentials, a refused token refresh, an invalid sync cursor, a push channel that cannot be renewed, or exhausted provider quota puts the connection in an unhealthy state that operators see in settings by name and that raises `calendar.connection-health-changed.v1`. An invalid cursor or a lapsed channel triggers a full resync before the next availability computation. While a required conflict or destination calendar is unreadable, public booking fails closed under `AVAIL-004` and no event type can be published under `EVENT-006`.
- **CAL-007 — Provider callbacks.** Calendar push channels are renewed before they expire. A callback authenticated under `AUTH-005` is processed independently of arrival order: duplicate or out-of-order delivery cannot duplicate a booking change or undo a newer one, and a callback describing a state older than the one held is discarded.
- **CAL-008 — External deletion and edits.** Deleting Dial's managed event in the destination calendar cancels the booking exactly once and sends the ordinary cancellation notices of `NOTIFY-004`. Any other external edit to the event's time or location is recorded as a sync conflict on the booking showing both times; the operator resolves it by restoring the managed event to the canonical time or by cancelling under `BOOK-009`, and Dial never adopts such an edit by itself. *Policy: `calendar.external-deletion.v1`; default: deletion cancels the booking.*
- **CAL-009 — Calendar file.** The confirmation and manage pages offer an `.ics` download for the booking carrying its stable UID and current occurrence. A reschedule increments that file's sequence and a cancellation marks the occurrence cancelled, so an invitee's calendar updates the same entry rather than gaining an unrelated one. The download makes no request to any provider not declared in `seed.json → externals`.

## Alternative availability requests

- **REQUEST-001 — Compose.** Where an event type enables it, an invitee can select future dates and time windows within the counts in `seed.json → limits`, whose starts precede their ends and which do not overlap each other, together with their IANA timezone, a name, an email address, and an optional note of at most 500 characters, and can review the exact request before submitting. A request that omits a date, a window, a name, or a valid email address, or that contains an overlapping or reversed window, is rejected with the offending field named and stores nothing.
- **REQUEST-002 — Submit.** A valid request is recorded once, acknowledged to the invitee on screen, and notified to operators within 60 seconds. The acknowledgement states the response expectation the operator configured, or, where none is configured, only that the host will follow up by email. Replaying the same nonce returns the existing request. A request reserves no time and creates no booking.
- **REQUEST-003 — Operator workflow.** Operators can list requests by submitted, handled, or closed state, open the invitee's windows rendered in both the invitee's and the host's zone, mark one handled or closed, and create a booking for an agreed time. That booking runs the ordinary `BOOK-012` path and marks the request handled.
- **REQUEST-004 — Routing.** A new request notifies every operator unless the routing policy selects a subset or suppresses email; suppressing email never suppresses the in-app record. *Policy: `availability-request.routing.v1`; default: every operator.*
- **REQUEST-005 — Invitee updates.** Submitting sends the invitee one acknowledgement email restating the requested windows in their own zone. Marking a request handled or closed sends the invitee exactly one further email; the closed one says the host cannot offer a time, and neither is sent twice however often the state is set again.

## Notifications

- **NOTIFY-001 — Booking confirmation.** Within 60 seconds of confirmation Dial emails, through `mail.sender.v1`, the invitee and every operator: the event, the time in each recipient's relevant zone, the duration, the answers, and, for operators, the current calendar state. Invitee mail omits host calendar setup details while naming a pending or unavailable joining link. The invitee's copy also carries their manage link and the `.ics` file of `CAL-009`; no operator copy contains the manage token. Where the meeting link is still pending the email says so instead of inventing one. An operator can mute booking mail — confirmations, changes, and reminders — for themselves in settings; the invitee's copy is never suppressed.
- **NOTIFY-002 — Link completion.** When a pending joining link becomes available Dial updates the managed calendar event and sends the invitee exactly one link-ready email, however many times the completion job runs.
- **NOTIFY-003 — Reminders.** A confirmed future booking schedules invitee and operator reminders carrying the event, the time in the recipient's zone, the location or join link, and, for the invitee, the manage link. A reminder is sent within 5 minutes of its scheduled offset. Rescheduling replaces the scheduled times and cancellation removes them, so no reminder is ever sent for a time the booking no longer holds. *Policy: `reminder.schedule.v1`; default: 24 hours and 1 hour before the start, scheduling only the offsets still in the future at confirmation or reschedule.*
- **NOTIFY-004 — Changes.** A reschedule or a cancellation sends the invitee and every operator exactly one email naming who made the change, the previous time, the new time or the cancellation reason, and, for operators, the current calendar state. Invitees receive relevant joining-link status without host calendar setup details. The one stated exception is `DATA-003`'s release of an imported booking that the source scheduler already cancelled: the source has told the invitee, so the release sends no email; the ordinary operator cancellation beside it still sends exactly one.
- **NOTIFY-005 — Delivery failure.** A transient email failure is retried up to 5 times over 1 hour. A permanent failure or a bounce is marked failed on the booking or request with a typed sanitised reason and safe remediation, and can be resent without duplicating a delivery that already succeeded. Raw provider errors are neither persisted nor rendered.
- **NOTIFY-006 — No mail loops.** Product mail is sent from a no-reply address. Mail delivered to that address is discarded and never creates a booking, a request, or a notification.
- **NOTIFY-007 — Connection alerts.** Within 60 seconds of the calendar connection becoming unhealthy under `CAL-006`, every operator is emailed once, naming the cause and the reconnect action, because public booking is failing closed meanwhile. This alert is operational and the mute of `NOTIFY-001` does not suppress it. No further alert is sent for that connection until it has recovered, and recovery is announced the same way.

## Data

- **DATA-001 — Export contents.** Supersedes `BASE-DATA-001` for credential and live-authority exclusions and documented canonicalization. `pnpm run export -- --output FILE` writes an owner-only archive outside tracked source; `pnpm run import -- --input FILE --yes` restores it only into a fully migrated empty install. The explicit `run` selects Dial’s script rather than pnpm’s built-in dependency importer. The archive contains every durable operator, host profile, host booking-link copy report, event type, question, availability rule and override, calendar-connection metadata and sync state, booking and occurrence, invitee answer and custom-field value, attendance mark, alternative request, delivery attempt, setting, and audit entry as JSON, plus every uploaded asset with its original bytes and content type. Unknown application tables fail closed. No OAuth access/refresh credential, session, sign-in/invitation link, setup/webhook secret, callback authority, private manage capability, or frozen capability-bearing mail is exported. Durable identities, consumed first-owner ownership, invitation cancellation guards and historical outcome records survive. Imported active operators sign in through new magic links; first-owner setup cannot be claimed again. Calendar health requires reauthorization under `CAL-006`, mail requires a fresh check, old private links remain invalid, and historical deliveries, deletion probes and cleanup targets cannot recreate provider effects. Export/import/export yields an equivalent entire archive under the explicit idempotent canonicalization in OPERATIONS.md; timestamps, IDs and original asset bytes are not discarded. Retain the source SESSION_SECRET privately outside the archive when transferring authenticated retry/erasure hash guards. Restore requires exclusive access to the empty destination; D1 and R2 are not jointly atomic; Base records owned object writes and commits D1 atomically, but an unsuccessful restore remains unpublished until recovered or repeated into a fresh empty install. Local transfer and native-owner authenticated hosted export are supported. Hosted export drains and fences application requests, background work and coordinator activity while capturing D1 and FILES, refuses pending erasure, expires abandoned maintenance without allowing expired jobs to download, and retains exact schema and original-salt checks at local import. Direct owner-account writes must remain quiesced. Queue messages and coordinator alarms are excluded execution authority; historical commands remain inert. Remote restore is not exposed. Legacy v2 archives are restored with their original revision before upgrading and re-exporting.
- **DATA-002 — Destructive personal erasure.** Deleting every booking and alternative request carrying an email address erases that person's invitee data from the deployment, including associated answers, notes, attendance, custom values, uploaded content and object metadata, private capabilities, local mail and reminders, provider facts, blocked identifiers, rate-limit identifiers, application logs and retained import copies. Each record can still be erased from its detail page; its manage link stops working, its managed calendar event is cancelled without sending the invitee a Dial email, and its audit survives with the invitee reference anonymised, as `BASE-DATA-003` requires. The email flow makes that existing deployment-wide guarantee explicit: an authenticated operator enters an email in Settings and receives a case-insensitive, write-free preview of matching bookings and requests. One confirmation erases every match within the request and returns an anonymous receipt with counts, completion time and separate calendar cleanup state. Success leaves no pending personal mail, reminders or import replay. Native entity deletion and its import custody, anonymous audit and portability fence commit together; a failed multi-record request may have completed earlier entities, issues no completed aggregate receipt, closes new admission for that address and is explicitly resumable with a fresh preview. Native calendar cleanup completes within five minutes when the maintained provider adapter answers; real provider failures remain visible. Old archives are refused by a destination retaining a trusted later erasure journal, including a journal carried by a post-erasure archive. An isolated old archive in a new empty store cannot know a future erasure. When the email also belongs to an operator, the same single confirmation erases all matching invitee records, including those of the acting or protected setup owner. The write-free preview and anonymous receipt explicitly report that the associated operator identity is retained; native accounts, sessions and sign-in authority remain usable. Operator identity removal is a separate OPER-002 action with its self, setup and last-owner holds. Invitee erasure never claims that a retained operator identity has been erased. Unrelated people, host configuration and shared assets remain. ERASURE.md records the complete storage census, restore procedure, implementation gaps and external-copy limits.

- **DATA-003 — Move-in import.** Operators preview saved scheduling responses without database writes, review per-kind losses and rejected overlaps at the preview upload instant (not file-selection time), download a schema-valid report, and acknowledge the reviewed fingerprint before a bounded, resumable import. The first preview of each selected upload captures its instant; repeated previews and owner choices retain that instant until the files are selected anew. Applying and resuming retain the exact reviewed instant. A server-issued upload identity bound to the owner, native session and source supplies fresh cryptographic randomness per booking before review, without preview writes; it expires after 30 minutes and is invalidated by an ended application. A new sign-in, including after archive restore, requires a fresh upload review; a runtime restart preserving the session does not. The upload identity grants no authority without the native operator session. The exact reviewed token hashes persist through apply and resume; a new application after undo receives new unsent capabilities. Imported event types remain drafts, and a missing supported private location has an actionable event-editor recovery while normal publication remains refused. Imported confirmed bookings retain their source location and answers, occupy their time across the host, are marked Imported, and create no mail, reminder, queue, calendar or meeting effects. Their private manage capability is unsent. Each imported creation records the authenticated importing operator, no previous start, and the imported start in native booking history. As a narrow exception to BOOK-010's action time for this imported creation only, its history time is the immutable reviewed snapshot as-of instant, explicitly labelled as effective snapshot time rather than application wall-clock time. The source creation time remains on the booking and original occurrence; the original provider creator is not inferred. OPER-004 shows this history and its time semantics. Later native cancellations and reschedules retain BOOK-010's actual action times; operator cancellation sends one ordinary NOTIFY-004 invitee email and leaves the source calendar entry alone. Conflict-preserving undo retains edited records and required parents. Identical source re-import is idempotent. DATA-002 erasure removes retained personal copies and prevents replay, withdrawing affected original artifacts while preserving other normalized records. Catch-up (extension): after an import is applied, a newer capture of the same source account previews as a catch-up. Records already saved by an applied import are matched by source identity and left out of its plan, so they are never duplicated, overwritten or saved a second time, including any edited, published, rescheduled or cancelled in Dial; the plan keeps only an extract of the records it reviews. New event types and future single-invitee bookings get the same losses, overlap rejections and choices as a first import and apply under their own saved job, resume and undo, with new unsent capabilities and no side effects; undo removes only what that catch-up added. A capture of a different source account, a capture taken while another import is unfinished, and records kept by a retained undo are refused with a plain message and no writes. The preview compares every still-future imported booking with the capture and lists one difference each: unchanged, moved at source (both times shown, including a source reschedule that cancels the old event for a rebooked invitee, which is not imported as a second booking), cancelled at source, invitee changed, or no longer in the capture. Nothing changes automatically. For a booking cancelled at source the operator may release this time: it cancels the Dial booking, frees its slot and buffers and records the operator and reason in history without emailing the invitee (the stated `NOTIFY-004` exception); the ordinary operator cancellation stays available beside it. Any other difference is kept with one recorded decision, or handled through the ordinary cancel or reschedule. Differences, capture times and old scheduling links are native rows: they appear in the schema-valid report (version 2; version 1 reports remain valid), survive reload and whole-install archive restore without restoring replay authority, and erasing a person removes their difference rows. Settings → Move in shows a switch-over checklist read from live data: each imported event type's old and Dial links with its status or exact publication reason and a downloadable list, the earliest day the source can be closed without stranding an invitee whose confirmation, manage link and reminders still belong to it, unresolved differences from the latest catch-up, the publication readiness of the destination calendar and sending address, and the date and age of the last capture. Dial never contacts or changes the source account. Validation is synthetic-only until an owner-supplied account export is checked.

## Out of scope

Declared in `seed.json` as non-goals and not tested: multiple hosts or workspaces; roles and permissions; team round-robin or collective scheduling; group events, classes, waitlists, or resource capacity; additional invitee guests on a booking; bookings that wait for host approval before they are confirmed; payments, deposits, refunds, or booking fees; recurring booking series or scheduling polls; marketplace discovery, ratings, or invitee-submitted reviews; SMS, WhatsApp, phone automation, CRM, campaigns, or marketing automation; automatic adoption of arbitrary external-calendar time or location edits; AI routing or ranking, `AVAIL-007` being deterministic and using no model; and native mobile apps.

## Internal custom fields

- **CUSTOM-001 — Operator annotations.** Operators define private fields for event types, bookings and availability requests in Settings, and edit their values on the corresponding record. Supported types are text (500 characters), long text (5000 characters), single choice (1–50 unique choices, each at most 120 characters) and checkbox. Labels contain 1–120 characters; definitions per entity type are bounded by `limits.maxCustomFieldsPerEntity`. The private routes validate entity ownership, field/entity matching, type, choice and size before writing. Booking annotations appear alongside invitee answers under OPER-004. Definitions and values are stored in extension-owned sidecars, reached through declared, typed source registry contributions and detail slots.
- **CUSTOM-002 — Annotation privacy and portability.** Custom definitions and values require a current operator session. They are never added to public documents, public API projections, outgoing mail or build-time browser source. Whole-install export and restore preserve definitions and values, including false checkboxes and multiline text; restore rejects invalid field types, references and choices before committing. Deleting a definition deletes its values; erasing a booking or request deletes its annotation sidecar; event deletion removes its own annotations without removing booking annotations. An additive upgrade preserves prior product data.
