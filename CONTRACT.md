# CONTRACT.md — Dial

Dial's observable product requirements. Each clause has a stable ID. This is the **target contract**: the scheduling product Dial is building towards, so a buyer of Calendly, Acuity Scheduling or Microsoft Bookings can switch, with each clause's current status recorded in the table at the end (`done`, `partial` or `missing`, judged from the code and tests at the cited paths). A `partial` or `missing` clause is a build obligation, not a claim. `pnpm run verify` runs the product checks; the manifest validator reports structural integrity only at the current maturity, not full behavioral acceptance. Security, privacy and operational requirements are in `BASELINE.md`; explicit supersessions are named below. Each clause's acceptance evidence is listed in `ACCEPTANCE.md`; verification evidence and uncovered journeys are recorded in HANDOVER.md. Workspace capacity (people, hosts, teams, event types and the booking volume the performance bounds hold at) is stated in `OPER-007` and wins over any lower value in `seed.json → limits`; rate limits and the other per-record counts are declared in `seed.json → limits`.

## Scope

Dial is a scheduling and appointment-booking platform for US independent professionals, service businesses and teams, replacing Calendly, Acuity Scheduling and Microsoft Bookings and going beyond Cal.com, SavvyCal and Reclaim. The complete job: hosts and teams publish booking links, team pages, routing forms and embeds; availability is checked across every calendar a host uses (Google, Microsoft 365/Outlook, iCloud) and booked into one destination with a Google Meet, Zoom, Teams, Webex or GoTo conference; events can be one-on-one, round-robin with priority and fairness, collective, group with capacity, recurring, approval-gated, single-use, offered-times or polled; invitees book in their own zone and language without an account, are asked by the host to pick a new time when plans change, add guests, pay or leave a deposit through the host's own Stripe or PayPal, and reschedule or cancel through a private link; invitees choose among offered durations and can land on the buyer's own thank-you page; each booking's mail reaches only its own hosts and invitee, and an invitee's reply reaches the host they booked, with calendar invitations and emails worded by the host's templates; workflows send email and consented SMS (through RER Managed SMS from day one, or the workspace's own Twilio) confirmations, reminders, follow-ups and no-show recovery on customizable timings; bookings flow to Orbit, HubSpot, Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Pipedrive, Intercom, Slack, Teams, Zapier, Make, Crank, webhooks and a public API; admins run roles, edit any member's hours and event types on their behalf, SSO/SCIM, an audit log, client records, analytics and exports; hosts work from the web, native iOS and Android apps, a Chrome extension and Gmail/Outlook add-ins; and owners move in from Calendly, Acuity, Microsoft Bookings, Cal.com and SavvyCal without re-notifying anyone. Agents carry the job past slot selection: they negotiate exceptions within the host's real constraints, follow up on unanswered threads, keep every calendar copy true, establish whether the meeting happened and prepare the agreed follow-through, always under a grant a person controls.

Dial is self-hosted: each deployment is owned by its operator, holds its data in its own Cloudflare account and exports all of it. A single host on a fresh install keeps the simple path: one profile, one calendar, one event and a link to share.

Sibling products stay separate and are linked, not rebuilt: Orbit is the CRM, Mango runs marketing email and SMS campaigns, Crank is general workflow automation, Pencil is general forms and surveys, Pane is the website builder, Nectar is subscription billing, Pantry is accounting, Auth is the workforce identity provider, Strap runs fitness and yoga studios (memberships, class packs, studio operations), Balm runs therapy and allied-health clinics (clinical scheduling, EHR, insurance), Placemat runs restaurant reservations, Lodge runs recruiting (and books interviews through Dial or its own scheduler), Lanyard runs hourly shift scheduling and Glove runs field-service dispatch.

## Authorization

- **AUTH-001 — Public authority.** Supersedes `BASE-ACCESS-004` for the paths listed in `seed.json → deploy.publicPaths` and for no others. Those paths authorize the caller by the capability material named in `AUTH-002` to `AUTH-007` instead of an operator session, each capability grants exactly the one action or record it names, and a request carrying no valid capability material changes nothing. Every other mutation requires a valid operator session, or an API credential under `AUTH-008`, and a server-side ownership and role check under `OPER-001`. Every `/app`, `/setup` and `/api/app` route sits behind one native-session check, so a request without a valid session reaches none of them. Until `OPER-001` ships every operator is an owner, so the session is the whole private check and `OPER-001` carries the host/team ownership and role check at this boundary; until `AUTH-008` ships no API credential exists, and `AUTH-008` carries the scoped-credential check and its boundary cases. *Acceptance:* `tests/operator-auth-boundary.test.mjs`, `tests/operator-management.test.mjs`, `tests/request-json-boundary.test.mjs`, `tests/portability-status.test.mjs` and `tests/booking-http.test.mjs` (each `[AUTH-001]`) drive anonymous, forged and cross-capability requests against the declared public paths and the private routes, including the `NOTIFY-009` invitation page `/manage/invite/:token` and its replacement-link command, where a booking manage token and an invitation token each open nothing on the other's surface; a new public surface joins this test and `seed.json → deploy.publicPaths` before it ships: routing forms (`ROUTE-001`), polls (`POLL-001`), single-use links (`AUTH-007`), payment returns (`PAY-001`) and workflow unsubscribe links (`FLOW-001`) each carry that step.
- **AUTH-002 — Booking nonce.** Reaching the details step for a selected slot, and reaching the review step of an alternative request, each issue a single-use nonce of at least 256 bits from a cryptographically secure random source, bound to the event type and to either the selected slot or the composed alternative request, and expiring 30 minutes after issue. The nonce is not bound to a browser: possession of an unexpired, unconsumed nonce is what authorizes the confirmation. A confirmation whose nonce is missing, unknown, expired, or bound to different values creates nothing and answers per `PUBLIC-003`; a nonce that has already been consumed is answered by `BOOK-003`. After an alternative request is submitted, that same consumed nonce grants read-only access to its acknowledgment delivery state, including after nonce expiry. This receipt grants no additional read or mutation beyond the existing confirmation-replay behavior of `BOOK-003` and `REQUEST-002`, which remains unchanged. The receipt returns only queued, sent, or failed, remains bound to the public event and original request, and becomes unavailable when either is unavailable or erased. *Acceptance:* `tests/booking-http.test.mjs`: issue, expiry, binding mismatch and replay of booking and request nonces through the public routes.
- **AUTH-003 — Booking access.** A booking is readable and changeable from a public surface only through the manage token defined in `BOOK-006`. A booking ID, an invitee email address, or any other guessable identifier grants access to no booking data. *Acceptance:* `tests/embed-document-authorization.test.mjs`: booking IDs and invitee email addresses grant nothing; only the manage token reads or changes a booking.
- **AUTH-004 — Calendar authorization callback.** The calendar OAuth callback accepts only signed state that is bound to the operator session that started the connection and was issued less than 10 minutes earlier. State that is missing, unsigned, mismatched, replayed, or expired stores no credential, connects no account, and returns 400. *Acceptance:* `tests/providers-runtime.test.mjs`: missing, unsigned, mismatched, replayed and expired OAuth state each return 400 and store nothing.
- **AUTH-005 — Provider callbacks.** A calendar or mail provider callback is accepted only when the provider's own authentication passes — its registered channel identity or its request signature — together with the secret configured for that provider. A callback that fails the check returns 401 and changes nothing. *Acceptance:* `tests/providers-runtime.test.mjs` and `tests/mail-connection.test.mjs`: calendar channel and SendGrid signature failures return 401 and change nothing.
- **AUTH-006 — First-owner admission.** On an empty native authentication store, a local CLI install accepts its setup token through the native Base bootstrap route. When managed owner-claim bindings are present, that route is closed and only a short-lived ES256 signed claim may admit the first owner. The issuer, application origin, installation identity, public verification keys and owner email come from trusted deployment configuration. Invalid claims write nothing. Successful admission atomically records a permanent consumed claim, creates the native owner and product identity, and issues a native session; concurrent losers roll back, and subsequent admission is refused after restart or removal of the original owner. Existing operator sessions, sign-in and equal-operator management retain their native protocol. *Acceptance:* `tests/owner-signin.test.mjs` and `tests/managed-admission.test.mjs`: setup-token and signed-claim admission, concurrent losers, restart and repeat refusal.

## Workspace, people and roles

- **OPER-001 — Roles and permissions.** A workspace has owners, admins, team managers, members (hosts) and read-only viewers. Owners and admins manage billing-free workspace settings, connections policy, people, teams, routing forms, workflows and integrations; team managers manage their teams' shared event types, round-robin pools and routing; members manage their own profile, calendars, availability, event types and bookings; owners and admins, and team managers for the members of their teams, also edit a member's schedules, overrides, time off and personal event types on that member's behalf under `OPER-006`; viewers read bookings and reports only. Every private route checks the caller's role and the record's owning host or team server-side. Wherever another clause says that an operator, operators or an admin acts, the acting roles are these, and every other role is refused: (a) **on a booking or request** (`BOOK-006` token rotation, `BOOK-009`, `BOOK-011`, `BOOK-012`, `BOOK-014`, `CAL-008`, `REQUEST-003`, `TEAM-007` and the agent grants of `AGENT-001`): its host, a person that host delegated to under `TEAM-007`, a team manager for bookings and requests of their teams' event types or members, and owners and admins for all; (b) **on a host's own settings** (profile and handle, event types, availability, limits, locked zone, embeds, redirect, calendar and conferencing connections): that host, plus the reach `OPER-006` gives owners, admins, team managers and event-type grantees; (c) **workspace-wide powers, owners and admins only**: inviting and removing people and changing roles below owner (`OPER-002`; a team manager changes only the membership of the teams they manage), email-wide and per-record erasure (`DATA-002`), every move-in, connection, preview, apply, catch-up, release, undo and switch-over sign-off (`DATA-003`, `MIGRATE-001` to `MIGRATE-005`), the email and domain block list (`PUBLIC-006`), custom-field definitions (`CUSTOM-001`), workspace analytics and ad-conversion tags (`INTEG-006`), branding and custom domain (`BRAND-001`), the sending address and deployment-wide reminder, change and notification policies, workflows beyond one host's own event types (`FLOW-001`), SSO and SCIM (`ADMIN-001`), API credentials beyond the caller's own records (`AUTH-008`), workspace integrations, and reading the audit log (`ADMIN-002`); (d) **owners only**: the owner editor (`EDITOR-001`), the whole-install archive export and restore (`DATA-001`), granting or removing the owner role and owner transfer. Viewers read bookings, requests and reports, export what `REPORT-003` allows them, and write nothing. A refused action returns 403 with the typed error `role_forbidden` naming the action; it makes no change, issues no editor grant, preview, upload identity, OAuth state or token, sends nothing, and writes exactly one `ADMIN-002` entry recording the refusal. A single-host install keeps today's experience: its operators are owners and every operator sees and acts on the same host profile, event types, availability, calendar connections, bookings and requests. Until this clause ships, owner is the only role and the owner acts for the admin role; the `role_forbidden` refusals stated by `PUBLIC-006`, `CUSTOM-001`, `EDITOR-001`, `BOOK-009`, `BOOK-011`, `BOOK-012`, `REQUEST-003`, `OPER-002` and `DATA-003`, each refusal's `ADMIN-002` entry and their role-scope Target steps are this clause's obligations, as is the host/team ownership and role check `AUTH-001` requires on every private mutation, and those clauses are done on their owner case. Booking mail follows these roles under `NOTIFY-008`: owners, admins and team managers receive another host's booking mail only through an opt-in copy for the scope they manage, which omits answers marked private and never carries the manage token; members receive none for another member's bookings, viewers none at all, and an alternative request on one host's event mails only that host; this clause carries those steps of `NOTIFY-008`'s two-host journey. A member sees only their own `REPORT-001` figures and drill-through; this clause carries that step of `REPORT-001`'s acceptance. Changing a person's role takes effect on their next request. A workspace holds the people, hosts, teams and event types of `OPER-007`, in any mix of roles. *Acceptance:* Operator-door journey with two hosts and a viewer: a member cannot edit another member's availability or read their bookings outside a shared team event; a viewer's write returns 403 with no change; a demoted admin loses settings on the next request. Role-scope journey with an owner, an admin, a manager of team T, member A (in T), a CSM C (host of booking X and request R) and a viewer: A and the viewer each try to open the owner editor, invite a person, remove C, change C's role, run an email-wide erasure preview for X's invitee and confirm it, erase X from its detail page, block a domain, create a custom-field definition, start a saved-file move-in preview and a Calendly connection, reschedule X, mark X attended, create a booking for C and list C's requests, and every step returns 403 `role_forbidden` with no change, no editor grant, no preview and no upload identity or OAuth state; the admin succeeds at every step except opening the owner editor and granting the owner role, which return 403 for the admin and succeed for the owner; the manager of T removes A from T, A keeps workspace access, and the manager's removal of A from the workspace returns 403; after each refusal the audit log holds exactly one new entry, the refusal; on a single-host install every operator is an owner and every step succeeds as today. Today `tests/operator-management.test.mjs` and `tests/local-setup-journey.test.mjs` prove the equal-operator single-host case only.
- **OPER-002 — Operator management.** Owners and admins invite and remove people and change roles in Settings; only an owner grants or removes the owner role. A team manager adds members to and removes them from the teams they manage, which changes team membership only and never removes a person from the workspace. Members and viewers invite, remove and change no one: each attempt returns 403 `role_forbidden` with no identity, session or token changed (`OPER-001`). Session or grant expiry, revocation, refused refresh and logout remove editor authority. ## Profiles, event types and links

- **PROFILE-001 — Host profile.** Each host edits their name, title, biography, avatar, IANA timezone, focus areas and social-proof entries, within the counts and lengths in `seed.json → limits`. A social-proof entry is a quotation and an attribution typed by an operator: Dial collects no invitee feedback and publishes no rating, score or count it did not receive from an operator. Each host has exactly one profile timezone, which is what every other clause means by "the host's zone" for that host's bookings; a team event's host zone is the assigned host's. The in-app preview renders the same content as the public page. `TEAM-001`, `TEAM-003` and `TEAM-005` each state their part of the multi-host rule in their own text. *Acceptance:* `scripts/verify-profile-authoring.mjs` (run by `tests/availability-diagnostics.test.mjs`) and `tests/runtime.test.mjs` prove the one-host profile, limits and a preview that shows the same fields as the public page; the target journey repeats it for two hosts in different zones and checks each host's slots and mail use their own zone.
- **PROFILE-002 — Public profile.** The host profile is served at `/book/<handle>`, where the handle is set by an operator. It shows the configured host content and every published, listed event type, and never a draft, unlisted, or disabled one. An operator can change the handle; the previous handle then answers per `PUBLIC-005`. *Acceptance:* `tests/booking-http.test.mjs`: the public profile lists only published listed events and an old handle answers 404.
- **PROFILE-003 — Avatar asset.** Supersedes `BASE-INPUT-003` for the `/media/` prefix only. The avatar is an upload within the size and type limits in `seed.json → limits`, stored in R2 and served under `/media/` with its declared content type and inline rather than with a download disposition, because it renders as an image on the public profile and event pages. That prefix is the public delivery channel the baseline requires a product contract to name; every other upload rule still applies, and no other upload in this product is publicly readable. *Acceptance:* `tests/avatar-upload.test.mjs` and `tests/avatar.test.mjs`: size and signature validation, inline `/media/` delivery with `nosniff`, replacement and removal.
- **EVENT-001 — Event type lifecycle.** Operators create, edit, publish, disable, and delete event types. An event type is draft, published, or disabled, and a published one is either listed on the public profile or unlisted and reachable only by its own URL. Each carries a title, description, one to four duration options of 5 to 480 minutes each with one of them the default (`EVENT-011`), color, location, and a slug unique among event types, and is served at `/book/<handle>/<slug>`. A slug can be edited; the previous slug is not retained and then answers per `PUBLIC-005`, while existing bookings and their manage links are unaffected. An operator can duplicate an event type: the copy is a draft with every setting, question, location, schedule binding, template (`NOTIFY-009`) and workflow attachment of the original, titled "<title> (copy)" with a new unique slug, and carries no bookings, requests or history. Creating or duplicating an event type past an event-type capacity of `OPER-007` is refused under that clause and creates nothing. `OPER-007`, `TEAM-006` and `FLOW-001` each state their part in their own text. *Acceptance:* `tests/runtime.test.mjs` and `tests/event-status-trigger.test.mjs`: create, edit, publish, disable and delete; `tests/booking-wire/duration-journey.mjs` edits the slug of an event that has a booking, and the old slug answers 404 (`PUBLIC-005`) while the new slug serves and the booking's manage link still works; `tests/booking-wire/duration-journey.mjs` and `tests/booking-wire/location-journey.mjs` exercise the native duration and duplication cases: saving five duration options, or none, is refused with the field named, and 30 and 60 minutes with 30 as the default are kept; duplicating a published event type with two questions, a buffer and an edited confirmation template creates a draft copy with the same settings, a new slug, no bookings and no public page until published; remaining capacity journey: a host at the per-host event-type capacity is refused a create and a duplicate with `workspace_capacity_reached` naming `event_types_per_host`, and no draft exists afterwards. *Implementation:* `tests/booking-wire/duration-journey.mjs`, `tests/booking-wire/location-journey.mjs`, `src/core/features/event-types/duplicate.ts`. *Remaining:* Native event settings, independent draft duplication including every location, question, schedule binding and correspondence template, and one to four durations. Workspace/host capacity, reusable team templates and workflow attachments are carried by `OPER-007`, `TEAM-006` and `FLOW-001`, which each name this clause.

- **EVENT-002 — Scheduling constraints.** Each event type configures its recurring availability, date-specific overrides, slot start increment of 5, 10, 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes, minimum notice of 0 minutes to 7 days, a booking horizon that is rolling (1 day to `seed.json → limits.availabilityLookaheadDays`, counted in calendar days or in weekdays), a fixed date range, or indefinite (up to that same lookahead limit), before and after buffers of 0 to 120 minutes each, and an optional maximum number of bookings per host day, where a host day is one calendar date in the host's timezone. ## Availability

- **AVAIL-001 — Schedule and overrides.** Recurring availability is a set of wall-clock windows per weekday in the host's IANA timezone. A date override replaces the recurring windows for one host date and may open or close that whole date. Editing a schedule or an override changes future availability only; it never alters a confirmed booking. *Acceptance:* `tests/availability-diagnostics.test.mjs`: weekday windows and date overrides change future availability only.
- **AVAIL-002 — Valid slots.** A slot is offered only when every one of these holds: its start falls on the event type's start increment measured from the start of its open window; the full duration fits inside that one window; the start is within the booking horizon and beyond the minimum notice; its host day is under the event type's booking limit; and it overlaps no booking in the `confirmed` state for this host on any event type and no busy interval from any selected conflict calendar, an external event the provider marks free being ignored. Dial's own managed event, read back from a conflict calendar where Dial last confirmed writing it, is the booking it represents rather than an external busy interval, so a canceled or moved booking releases its old time under `BOOK-007` and `BOOK-008` without waiting for the provider or its cached busy data. Anything else is not offered. *Acceptance:* `tests/dst-native.test.mjs`, `tests/booking-wire.test.mjs` and `tests/providers-d1.test.mjs`: every validity rule, free-marked external events ignored and Dial's own managed event not double-counted.
- **AVAIL-003 — Buffers.** The event type's before and after buffers extend the interval that must be free around a candidate slot, over both confirmed Dial bookings and external busy intervals. A buffer never changes the invitee's start or end time, the duration shown anywhere, or the times on the calendar event and invitation. *Acceptance:* `tests/availability-diagnostics.test.mjs`: buffers exclude adjacent times without changing displayed or calendar times.
- **AVAIL-004 — Freshness and failure.** Availability is recomputed when the event page or a week loads. Changing the invitee's timezone re-renders and re-ranks the same slots and needs no new calendar read, because slot validity does not depend on the invitee's zone. A displayed slot may rest on cached provider busy data no more than 5 minutes old; the final check in `BOOK-001` uses provider data no more than 60 seconds old. With no connected calendar, saved schedule rules and persisted Dial bookings are authoritative and no provider read occurs. This does not relax checks on an unhealthy, incomplete or revoked connection. Every selected conflict calendar is required, and a provider read that does not complete within 5 seconds counts as unreadable. If a required calendar is unreadable at either point, Dial offers and confirms no slot for that host and tells the invitee that booking is temporarily unavailable, rather than offering unverified time. *Acceptance:* `tests/lifecycle-journey.test.mjs` (journey 6) and `tests/host-setup-journey.test.mjs`: unreadable calendars fail closed and recover; calendar-optional events read no provider.
- **AVAIL-005 — Timezones.** Every invitee-facing time is rendered in the invitee's selected timezone, which starts as the browser's IANA zone when the browser reports one and as the host's zone when it does not. The zone is resolved in the browser and slot instants are fetched in UTC, so no visitor-derived value reaches the server on page load, as `BASE-PUBLIC-001` requires: the invitee's zone is sent only with a request their own action causes — changing the zone, moving week, or selecting a slot. The invitee can change the zone at any point before confirmation. The selected-day group names the invitee's zone once, and every selectable time carries its UTC offset so repeated wall-clock times remain distinct. Other invitee-facing times carry their zone name and UTC offset. In the selected-day picker, host time is shown only when the host's zone differs from the invitee's, including the host date when the local dates differ. The details and confirmation surfaces also show the host's local time, including when the zones match, preserving the receipt's both-zone requirement in `BOOK-005`. A confirmed booking stores the UTC instant and both the host's and the invitee's IANA zone identifiers. *Acceptance:* `tests/booking-axe.test.mjs` (timezone stage) and `tests/booking-axe/timezone.mjs`: browser-resolved zone, offsets on every time, host time where zones differ.
- **AVAIL-006 — Daylight saving time.** A local time that does not exist during a forward transition is never offered. Repeated wall-clock times during a backward transition are shown with distinct UTC offsets, so each offered time maps to exactly one instant. A DST transition between confirmation and the meeting never moves a booking's stored UTC instant, and the host and invitee renderings each follow their own zone's rules. *Acceptance:* `tests/dst-native.test.mjs`: forward-gap times never offered, repeated times carry distinct offsets, stored instants never move.
- **AVAIL-007 — Recommendations and filters.** At most 5 recommended times are shown, each a slot already valid under `AVAIL-002` and each showing the reason it was chosen. The ordering is deterministic: the same inputs always produce the same order. The browser ranks the verified offers in both the host's and invitee's selected zones from the first display. It uses a free-time ordering supplied with the offers, without sending the device zone on page load or disclosing private occupancy intervals; action-triggered server reads use the selected zone under `AVAIL-005`. An invitee-facing filter only narrows or reorders slots already valid under `AVAIL-002` and can never surface one that is not. *Policy: `availability.recommendation.v1`; default: slots fully inside 09:00–17:00 in both the host's and the invitee's selected zone first, then the larger sum of contiguous free minutes immediately before and after the slot, then the earlier UTC start.* *Acceptance:* `tests/booking-axe/day-picker.mjs`: at most five deterministic recommendations with reasons, filters never surface an invalid slot.
- **AVAIL-008 — Response time.** Loading one week of availability for one event type returns within 2 seconds, for an event type of any kind, including a round-robin event type pooling 25 hosts and a collective event type of 10 hosts, on a single-host install and on a workspace at the `OPER-007` capacity alike. *Acceptance:* `tests/booking-axe/acceptance-capacity.mjs`: one week of availability for one event type within 2 seconds on the capacity fixture. Target: on the `OPER-007` capacity fixture (300 hosts with 6 personal event types each, 50 team event types, 1,000,000 bookings), one week of a one-on-one event type, a 25-host round-robin event type and a 10-host collective event type each loads within 2 seconds.
- **AVAIL-009 — Operator diagnostics.** For any time an operator evaluates, Dial names which rule excluded it — schedule, date override, start increment, minimum notice, horizon, buffer, host-day limit, an existing Dial booking, or a conflict-calendar busy interval — without disclosing external event detail that `CAL-002` does not retain. *Acceptance:* `tests/availability-diagnostics.test.mjs`: each exclusion rule is named for an evaluated time without external event detail.

## Booking lifecycle

- **BOOK-001 — Final validation.** Confirming a booking revalidates every answer against the event type's current questions and rechecks the selected slot under `AVAIL-002` against persisted Dial bookings and, when connected, a fresh conflict-calendar read. Any failure creates no booking, no calendar event, and no email, and names the reason: an invalid or missing answer returns 400, and a slot that is no longer valid returns 409. *Acceptance:* `tests/lifecycle-journey.test.mjs` (journeys 2 and 6) and `tests/availability-diagnostics.test.mjs`: invalid answers return 400 and a no-longer-valid slot 409, with no side effects.
- **BOOK-002 — Atomic claim.** Every slot admits at most one booking in the `confirmed` state. Claiming the slot and recording its applicable calendar, meeting, and notification commands happen in one transaction that either commits entirely or leaves nothing behind. Of any number of concurrent confirmations for the same slot exactly one commits; every other returns 409 with a `slot_taken` code and is returned to the choose-time step with availability recomputed. *Acceptance:* `tests/booking-lifecycle.test.mjs`: 50 concurrent claims yield one booking and `slot_taken` for the rest.
- **BOOK-003 — Idempotent confirmation.** Replaying a confirmation with an already-consumed nonce and an identical payload returns the stored result of the original booking and creates no second booking, calendar event, meeting link, or email. Replaying it with a different payload returns 409 and creates nothing. *Acceptance:* `tests/lifecycle-journey.test.mjs` (journey 5): replayed nonces return the stored receipt; a changed payload is refused.
- **BOOK-004 — States and occurrences.** A booking is confirmed, canceled, or rescheduled, and has one or more occurrences of which exactly one is current: a reschedule closes the current occurrence and opens a new one under the same booking ID and iCalendar UID. The calendar event, the meeting link, and each email carry their own pending, succeeded, or failed state; none of them changes the booking's state, and no external failure silently cancels or moves a booking. *Acceptance:* `tests/occurrence-mail-duration.test.mjs` and `tests/booking-wire.test.mjs`: occurrences and independent side-effect states.
- **BOOK-005 — Confirmation receipt.** Unless the event type or routing route redirects the invitee under `BOOK-013`, within 2 seconds of a successful claim the invitee is shown the event, the start and end in their own zone and in the host's, the duration, the host, the location or its pending state, the calendar file of `CAL-009`, and the manage link of `BOOK-006`. The join control is enabled only when a provisioned joining URL is stored on the booking; while `CAL-003` is still pending it is disabled and labeled as pending. *Acceptance:* `tests/booking-wire.test.mjs` and `tests/booking-axe/booking.mjs`: receipt contents and the pending join control.
- **BOOK-006 — Manage link.** Every confirmed booking has a manage link whose token carries at least 256 bits from a cryptographically secure random source and grants view and change access to that one booking, without creating an invitee account. The token is transmitted only to the invitee, in their own email and on their confirmation page; operator surfaces show the booking but never the token, offering an action that copies or resends the link instead. A reschedule keeps the token, which always resolves to the booking's current occurrence. The token expires 90 days after the booking's start instant, and an operator can rotate it, which invalidates the previous token immediately. A request with an expired, rotated, or unknown token answers per `PUBLIC-003` and discloses no booking data. *Acceptance:* `tests/embed-document-authorization.test.mjs` and `tests/booking-http.test.mjs`: token entropy, operator surfaces without the token, rotation and expiry.
- **BOOK-007 — Invitee rescheduling.** The holder of a booking's manage link can move it to another slot on the same event type. The new slot is validated under `BOOK-001` with only this booking's own occurrence excluded from internal conflicts, then claimed under `BOOK-002` before the old slot is released; if any step fails, the original booking is unchanged and still confirmed. *Policy: `booking.reschedule.v1`; default: any number of reschedules, allowed until the booking's start.* *Acceptance:* `tests/lifecycle-journey.test.mjs` (journey 3) and `tests/booking-axe.test.mjs`: reschedule over its own time, reminders replaced, `.ics` SEQUENCE raised, one event moved.
- **BOOK-008 — Invitee cancellation.** The holder of a booking's manage link can cancel it with an optional reason. Cancellation cannot be reversed, releases the slot and its buffers within 5 seconds and before the next availability computation, and repeating it returns the same result without sending a second notification. *Policy: `booking.cancellation.v1`; default: allowed until the booking's start.* *Acceptance:* `tests/lifecycle-journey.test.mjs` (journey 4) and `tests/booking-axe.test.mjs`: the slot is offered again at once and a repeat cancel sends nothing.
- **BOOK-009 — Operator changes.** A person who may act on the booking under `OPER-001` (its host, a person the host delegated to under `TEAM-007`, a manager of a team the booking's event type or host belongs to, and owners and admins for every booking) can reschedule or cancel any confirmed booking under `BOOK-002`, `BOOK-007`, `BOOK-008`, `BOOK-010`, `CAL-003`, and `NOTIFY-004`. The reschedule and cancellation policies constrain invitees only: an operator may also act on a booking that has already started, and may reschedule onto a time outside published availability after confirming that override explicitly, but never onto a time that overlaps another confirmed booking. An imported booking that a catch-up lists as canceled at the source may instead be released under `DATA-003`. Instead of choosing the new time, the operator can ask the invitee to pick one under `BOOK-014`. A reschedule keeps the booking's host; handing a booking to another host is a reassignment under `TEAM-007`. A member acting on another host's booking without that delegation, and any viewer, gets 403 `role_forbidden` with the booking, calendar event and mail unchanged. Until `OPER-001` ships every operator is an owner and the owner acts for the admin role; the `role_forbidden` refusals above, their `ADMIN-002` entries and the role-scope Target below are `OPER-001`'s obligations. *Acceptance:* `tests/operator-change.test.mjs` and `tests/move-in-booking-actions.test.mjs`: operator reschedule and cancel, explicit availability override, never over another booking. Target, carried by `OPER-001`: in the `OPER-001` role-scope journey a member's and a viewer's reschedule and cancel of another host's booking each return 403 with nothing changed or sent, while the host, the manager of the booking's team and the admin each succeed.
- **BOOK-010 — History.** Rescheduling records the original occurrence as rescheduled and links it to its replacement. Every creation, cancellation, and reschedule records who acted — the manage-link holder, the named operator, or the calendar provider under `CAL-008` — the time, the previous and new start, and any reason given. *Acceptance:* `tests/booking-wire.test.mjs`: actor, time, previous and new start and reason for each change, including the provider actor.
- **BOOK-011 — Attendance.** After a booking has ended, a person who may act on it under `OPER-001` (its host, a delegate under `TEAM-007`, a manager of its team, or an owner or admin) can mark it attended or a no-show and add a note. ## Calendars and meetings

- **CAL-001 — Calendar connections.** Each host connects one or more calendar accounts through OAuth under `AUTH-004`: Google Calendar (`CAL-001` to `CAL-009`), Microsoft 365 and Outlook.com (`CAL-010`) and iCloud or another CalDAV server (`CAL-011`), up to the account and calendar counts in `seed.json → limits`. Across all of a host's accounts they select the calendars whose busy intervals block availability (`limits.maxConflictCalendars`) and exactly one calendar to receive Dial's bookings. A connection belongs to its host; reconnecting replaces it and keeps the selections, and the host or an admin can disconnect. Calendar connection is optional for fixed locations. After an explicit disconnect, sync stops and the connection shows as disconnected. Fixed-location published event types keep offering times from saved availability minus existing bookings; provider-conference events offer no slots until their provider is restored. Existing managed bookings retain their calendar obligation and cannot be rescheduled while disconnected; their history is unchanged. Each provider is implemented behind `calendar.provider.v2`. *Acceptance:* Today `tests/lifecycle-journey.test.mjs` (journey 1) and `tests/providers-d1.test.mjs` prove one Google account per deployment with multiple conflict calendars and one destination. The target journey connects a Google and a Microsoft account to one host, takes conflicts from both, writes to the chosen destination, and disconnects one account with the documented effects.
- **CAL-002 — Busy privacy.** A calendar read retains only the identifiers, time bounds, transparency, and deletion state needed for conflict detection and sync. - **CAL-003 — Managed event.** A booking made without a connected calendar durably records calendar delivery as not required, schedules no calendar commands on creation, reschedule, cancellation or erasure, and never claims provider success. For a claim using a connected calendar, within 60 seconds the destination calendar holds one event for the booking carrying the title and description rendered from the event type's invitation templates (`NOTIFY-009`), the booking's start and end with explicit timezone data, a stable iCalendar UID, the configured location or a Google Meet conference provisioned for this booking, and the invitee as an attendee so the provider's own invitation and updates also reach them — or the booking's calendar state is pending and `CAL-005` applies. - **CAL-004 — Idempotent side effects.** Every calendar and meeting command carries the booking and occurrence IDs as its provider idempotency key. - **API-002 — Webhooks.** Admins subscribe HTTPS endpoints to booking created, rescheduled, reschedule requested (`BOOK-014`), canceled, no-show, attendance, payment, routing submission and poll events at workspace, team or host scope. This clause also owns subscriber and webhook dispatch of CAL-006’s natively recorded `calendar.connection-health-changed.v1` facts, retaining their event IDs, states, named reasons and times. Each delivery is signed (HMAC-SHA256 with a timestamp), retried with backoff for 24 hours, shown with its status and response code, and redeliverable by hand; payloads carry no manage token and carry the booking's UTM and source values (`LINK-003`) and its chosen duration (`EVENT-011`). *Cites:* BASE-SECRET-001; BASE-DATA-001 as superseded by DATA-001; BASE-DATA-003 as specified by DATA-002; BASE-DATA-004. *Authority:* Native workspace owners only until `OPER-001` supplies the wider administration model; the conservative owner check remains server-side. *Acceptance:* Native test against a local receiver: each event, including a recorded calendar health transition, delivers once to its subscriber with a valid signature, a 500 retries on schedule and succeeds once, manual redelivery repeats with the same event ID, a booking payload carries its UTM and source values (`LINK-003`), a booking payload carries the chosen duration (`EVENT-011`), and a deleted subscription receives nothing.
- **API-003 — Machine-credential booking API.** An agent or another product (for example Handset) books, reschedules and cancels through Dial with a scoped bearer credential and no operator session. This clause adds to `AUTH-008`, `API-001` and `API-002` and weakens none of them. *Credentials.* An owner or admin (`OPER-001`; until it ships, the owner) creates a machine credential in Settings under a fresh operator session. It has a name; a display name that invitee-facing notices use for the machine actor (default: the host's name); scopes from the `AUTH-008` set, where read bookings and write bookings are independent and neither implies the other, plus read availability; an allow-list of event types and an allow-list of host calendars (`CAL-001`) it may reach, each empty meaning none and never all; a required expiry no later than `seed.json → limits.maxCredentialLifetimeDays`; and an attestation, off by default, that the calling application collects SMS consent itself (see below). The secret is at least 256 bits from a cryptographically secure source, carries the fixed prefix `dial_` so secret scanners can find it, is shown once at creation and once at each rotation, and is stored only as a hash; Settings, the API and every log line show only the credential ID and the first 8 characters. A credential can reach only event types and calendars the creating person may act on under `OPER-001`, re-checked on every request, so demoting or removing the creator (`ADMIN-001`) stops it on the next request. The creating person and every later change are recorded. Rotation issues a second secret and keeps the old one valid for an overlap the owner sets up to 24 hours (default 0); expiry, revocation and the end of an overlap take effect on the next request. The credential list shows each credential's name, prefix, scopes, allow-lists, creation, expiry, rotation state, last-used time and revoked state; a credential that has never been used shows no last-used time. A credential never creates credentials, widens a scope, approves or declines a request (`EVENT-008`), marks a payment, charges, refunds, overrides price, minimum notice, horizon, conflict or buffer checks (`BOOK-012`'s operator overrides are not available), edits event types, or touches export, erasure or roles. It is accepted only in an `Authorization: Bearer` header over HTTPS, never in a query string or body, and Dial sends no CORS headers on `/api/v1`, so a browser page cannot hold one. *Doors,* all under `/api/v1` and checked at the `AUTH-001` boundary: `GET /credential` (the caller's own name, scopes, allow-lists, expiry and remaining rate allowance); `GET /event-types` and `GET /event-types/{id}/availability?start&end&timezone` (the same slots as the public page under `AVAIL-002`, hiding everything the credential's allow-lists exclude); `POST /bookings`; `GET /bookings` (cursor, filter by state, event type and start range) and `GET /bookings/{id}`; `POST /bookings/{id}/reschedule`; `POST /bookings/{id}/cancel`; `GET`, `POST` and `DELETE /webhook-subscriptions`; `GET /webhook-deliveries` and `POST /webhook-deliveries/{id}/replay`. A booking resource names its state, occurrences, event type, host, invitee answers, chosen duration, payment and approval state, `created_by` (type and credential name) and never a manage token. *Booking rules.* A create runs the same validation, atomic claim and side effects as the public flow (`BOOK-001`, `BOOK-002`, `AVAIL-002`): the invitee gets the ordinary `NOTIFY-001` mail and manage link, a blocked email or domain (`PUBLIC-006`) is refused exactly as the public flow refuses it and creates nothing, and an event type that needs host approval (`EVENT-008`) answers 202 with the booking `pending_approval` and the slot held, confirming only when the host approves. A priced, deposit or saved-card-fee event type (`PAY-001`, `PAY-003`, `PAY-007`) answers 202 `payment_required` with the hosted payment step and the booking confirms only on the signed processor event under `AUTH-005`; the credential cannot set, waive or reduce an amount, choose a method the event type forbids, or mark anything paid, and a priced approval type keeps the `PAY-007` authorization and decision rules. A request carries SMS consent only as an object naming the disclosure text version, acceptance and capture time; Dial accepts it only from a credential whose owner attested that its application collects consent, records the credential and disclosure version as the source, and otherwise refuses the object with 422 `consent_not_permitted` and sends no SMS; an opted-out number (`FLOW-002`) stays suppressed whatever the request says. Reschedule follows `BOOK-007` and cancel follows `BOOK-008` (cancellation is irreversible, repeats return the stored result and notify once, and refund or fee follows `PAY-003` policy exactly as for an invitee); both refuse a booking outside the credential's scope. *Idempotency.* Every POST that creates or changes a booking requires an `Idempotency-Key` of 16 to 128 characters, scoped to the credential and kept 24 hours. The same key with a canonically identical payload returns the stored result with the original status and `Idempotent-Replay: true` and creates no second booking, mail, calendar event or webhook; the same key with a different payload returns 409 `idempotency_key_reused` and creates nothing; a concurrent request on a key still in flight returns 409 `request_in_progress` with `Retry-After` and creates nothing; a missing key returns 400 `idempotency_key_required`. *Errors and limits.* Every refusal is JSON `{error: {code, message, request_id}}` with a stable code: 401 `invalid_credential`, `credential_expired`, `credential_revoked`; 403 `scope_forbidden` (the credential lacks the scope for this action); 404 `not_found` for any booking, event type or calendar outside its allow-lists or workspace, identical to a record that does not exist; 409 and 422 per above and per the booking rules with the named reason of the failed rule; 429 `rate_limited` with `Retry-After` and `X-RateLimit-Limit`, `-Remaining` and `-Reset` headers on every response, with separate per-credential limits for reads, for writes and for failed authentications (the failed-authentication limit also applies per source address under `BASE-ACCESS-003`), set in `seed.json → limits`; a limited or refused request changes nothing. *Webhooks.* A credential with the webhooks scope manages only subscriptions that deliver events for its own allow-listed event types and calendars, and a subscription's payloads never include another event type's bookings. Endpoints must be HTTPS and are refused if they resolve at subscription time or at each delivery to a private, loopback, link-local or metadata address. Each subscription has its own signing secret, shown once and rotatable with the overlap rule above; each delivery carries `Dial-Signature: t=<unix>,v1=<HMAC-SHA256 of t.body>`, a unique event ID, a per-booking sequence number so receivers can order and drop stale events, and the timestamp window (5 minutes) that receivers are told to enforce. Delivery, retry for 24 hours and manual redelivery stay as `API-002` says; a replay through the API re-sends the same event ID and body with a fresh timestamp and signature, works only for deliveries of the credential's own scope within 30 days, and refuses an event whose booking has been erased with 410 `erased`. A subscription whose endpoint fails for 24 hours of retries is disabled with a named reason, an `ADMIN-002` entry and one notice to the credential's owner. *Actor and audit.* Every booking created, rescheduled or canceled by a credential records the credential as the actor in `BOOK-010` history with the credential's ID and name at that time; the `NOTIFY-004` notice names the credential's display name as who made the change; and `ADMIN-002` records credential creation, scope or allow-list change, rotation, revocation, expiry, the last use before revocation, each webhook subscription change, endpoint disabling, and, collapsed to one entry per credential, reason and minute, each refused call (`scope_forbidden`, `credential_revoked`, `credential_expired`, `rate_limited`). Entries keep the credential's name after it is deleted and keep a stable opaque ID. *No bypass.* Nothing here relaxes `PAY-007` approval, payment authorization, `FLOW-002` consent, `PUBLIC-006` blocks, the `AUTH-003` rule that a manage token is the only public booking authority (no response, webhook, log or notice carries one for the credential's use), or `DATA-002` erasure; erasing an invitee deletes that person's data from stored idempotency results, webhook delivery bodies and replayable payloads, leaves a tombstone so a replayed idempotency key returns 410 `erased` and creates nothing, and keeps the anonymized audit entries. *Archive and restore.* Under `DATA-004`, the `DATA-001` archive carries each credential's metadata (ID, name, display name, prefix, scopes, allow-lists, creation, expiry, rotation, last-used and revoked state) and each webhook subscription's endpoint and scope, and excludes every secret, secret hash, signing secret and idempotency record; restoring into an empty install brings every credential back revoked with reason `restored_from_archive` and every subscription back disabled until its signing secret is rotated, so no archive and no restore ever carries or recreates machine authority. *Acceptance:* API test at the HTTP door against a local runtime with providers synthetic at the edge, asserting each of these; ## Push further: agents on the scheduling job

Today's up-and-comers stop at slot selection (Cal.com, SavvyCal), rearrange flexible blocks (Reclaim) or draft replies in beta (Calendly's assistant). Dial's agents own the outcome after the link is sent: the meeting is agreed, every copy is true, it happened, and the agreed follow-up is under way. Each clause names what the agent does, on which data, and under what human control; `AGENT-001` governs all of them.

- **AGENT-001 — Agent authority, limits and audit.** Every agent in this section acts only under a grant a host or admin gives: which agent, for which hosts, event types and invitees, which actions (draft, send, hold, book, reschedule, cancel; never charge), how many contacts per person and per day, quiet hours, an expiry, and whether each external message needs approval first. Every proposal and action is recorded with the data it used, the rule it followed and the person who approved it; hosts see a queue of pending proposals, can approve, edit or reject each, and can pause any agent at once. Agents never move a confirmed meeting, charge money, mark attendance, or send to a person who opted out, without the specific authority this clause names. *Acceptance:* Native test: an agent without a grant proposes but sends nothing; an expired grant stops the next action; pausing stops queued sends within one scheduled pass; every action appears in the admin audit log (`ADMIN-002`) with its approver.
- **AGENT-002 — Exception negotiator.** When an invitee needs a time outside published availability (an alternative request, a reply to a confirmation, or a routing submission marked urgent), the agent reads the host's real constraints (working hours, calendars across every account, flexible holds the host marked movable, travel buffers, booking caps) and the invitee's stated windows, and proposes up to three feasible exception times to the host with the trade-off of each. With approval it emails the invitee those times as `LINK-002` offered times, holds them for the granted window, and books the accepted one under `BOOK-012`. It never offers a time the host did not approve. *Acceptance:* Native test on a seeded host: an alternative request outside hours yields three proposals that each satisfy every constraint, the host approves two, the invitee receives exactly those two, accepting one books it and releases the other.
- **AGENT-003 — Unanswered-thread follow-up.** For offered times, alternative requests, polls and approval requests that are still open, the agent sends at most the granted number of follow-ups at the granted spacing, adapts to replies (a new window, a decline, a delegate's address), closes the thread when the person books or declines, and tells the host when it stops and why. It reads only Dial's own records and replies sent to the agent's thread address under the host's grant; that address is separate from the product sender of `NOTIFY-006`, which Dial still never reads. *Acceptance:* Native test: an unanswered offer gets one follow-up after 2 days and a final one after 5, booking in between cancels the rest, a decline closes the thread with one note to the host, and the contact limit is never exceeded.
- **AGENT-004 — Calendar drift reconciler.** The agent continuously compares each booking's canonical time, host, attendees and conference with every calendar copy and conference provider: events moved or deleted outside Dial, duplicate events after a provider outage, missing conferences, stale attendee lists after reassignment, revoked tokens and lapsed subscriptions. It fixes unambiguous drift Dial owns (re-creating a missing conference, removing a duplicate it created), and for ambiguous external edits proposes restore, adopt-with-confirmation or cancel to the host, then verifies the result on the provider. *Acceptance:* Native test with provider fixtures: a duplicate created by a lost response is removed, a missing Zoom meeting is re-created with one link-ready email, an external time edit yields a proposal and adopting it after approval sends one change notice. Today `CAL-008` sync conflicts and `CAL-004` idempotent reconciliation are proven by `tests/booking-wire.test.mjs`.
- **AGENT-005 — Meeting happened check.** After each meeting the agent assembles the evidence of whether it happened (conferencing attendance facts from `MEET-004`, the host's calendar response, a cancellation or late reschedule, the host's notes) and proposes attended, no-show, late or rescheduled-in-meeting to the host, who confirms the mark. Unconfirmed proposals never become marks, and missing evidence is reported as unknown. *Acceptance:* Native test: a Zoom report with the invitee present proposes attended, an empty report proposes no-show with the evidence listed, and only the host's confirmation writes `BOOK-011`. Today hosts mark attendance by hand under `BOOK-011`.
- **AGENT-006 — No-show recovery.** For a confirmed no-show, the agent drafts a recovery message that uses the meeting's stated purpose and the invitee's answers, offers a few real open times, and applies the host's no-show policy (deposit required next time, fee only with explicit host confirmation). It stops when the invitee books, declines, opts out or reaches the contact limit, and never turns scheduling consent into marketing consent. *Acceptance:* Native test: a no-show produces one approved message with valid times, a rebooking closes the recovery, an opt-out prevents any message, and no fee is charged without the host's confirmation.
- **AGENT-007 — Meeting follow-through.** With every participant's consent recorded, the agent reads the meeting's transcript or recap from Zoom, Teams or Meet (or a recording service the host connects), drafts a recap with the decisions and action items it can cite to transcript lines, and proposes the agreed follow-up: the recap email, the next meeting at the agreed cadence as offered times, owner reminders for inputs due before it, and the CRM update. Nothing is sent or booked until the host approves, and the agent never invents an action item without a citation. *Acceptance:* Native test on a fixture transcript: every proposed action item links to a transcript span, an uncited claim is refused by the checker, the host's edits are what is sent, and the agreed next meeting is offered as times the invitee books.
- **AGENT-008 — Pre-meeting brief.** Before each meeting the agent prepares a brief for the host from data the host granted: the invitee's answers, previous meetings and follow-through status in Dial, routing outcome, and the matched record in Orbit, HubSpot or Salesforce, with each point linked to its source. It flags missing intake answers and asks the invitee for them through the manage link when the host allows. *Acceptance:* Native test: the brief cites each fact to its source record, omits sources the grant excludes, and a missing required document triggers one approved request to the invitee.
- **AGENT-009 — Scheduling assistant in email threads.** A host copies Dial's assistant address (a dedicated inbound address, separate from the unread product sender of `NOTIFY-006`) on an email thread; the assistant reads only that thread, proposes times from the host's real availability in the thread's language and zone, handles replies and counter-proposals, and books when a time is agreed, sending the invitation and updating the thread. The host approves the first message per thread unless their grant allows the assistant to send directly. *Acceptance:* Native test with an inbound-mail fixture: a CC'd thread receives three valid times after host approval, a counter-proposal inside availability books directly, and a thread from an unknown sender is ignored.
- **AGENT-010 — Flexible holds and availability tuning.** Hosts mark some calendar blocks as flexible (focus time, admin, travel prep that Dial created); for a high-priority event type the agent may move a flexible block inside its allowed range to open a requested time, then re-place the block, never touching other events. Separately, it reviews booking data monthly and proposes availability, buffer and limit changes (for example widening Tuesday afternoons that fill first, or adding buffers after long meetings that run over), which the host accepts or declines. *Acceptance:* Native test: a priority booking moves one flexible block within its range and re-places it, a non-flexible event is never moved, and a monthly proposal changes nothing until accepted.
- **AGENT-011 — Voice booking through a contracted provider.** Hosts may connect a voice provider (Twilio Programmable Voice with an AI voice service the host contracts) so callers can book, reschedule or cancel by phone and receive reminder calls. Dial supplies availability, booking and manage actions through `API-001`; the call identifies itself as automated, follows the host's grant and TCPA rules for outbound calls, and every booking it makes is an ordinary booking with an SMS or email confirmation. Dial is not a phone system: numbers, IVR and recording live with the provider. *Acceptance:* Integration test with the voice provider's webhooks synthetic: a call transcript requesting Tuesday at 3 p.m. books a valid slot once, an unavailable time is answered with alternatives, and an outbound reminder call is not placed without recorded consent.

## Honest boundaries

What Dial does not do, and how a buyer gets it:

- **Calendar and conferencing providers.** Dial is not a calendar or video service and cannot guarantee another provider's uptime. It uses Google Calendar, Microsoft Graph, CalDAV, Zoom, Webex and GoTo APIs under the host's own accounts, shows connection health, and fails closed rather than offering unverified time.
- **Phone system, SMS network and meeting recording.** Dial is not a carrier, phone system, IVR or notetaker. SMS goes through RER Managed SMS or the workspace's own Twilio account and registered campaign (`FLOW-006`, `FLOW-002`); voice booking through a voice provider the host contracts (`AGENT-011`); transcripts and recordings come from Zoom, Teams, Meet or a recording service the host connects (`AGENT-007`). Business phone systems are not a Dial product.
- **Payment processing.** Dial never touches card numbers or holds funds. Payments, refunds, payouts, disputes and US sales-tax calculation run in the host's own Stripe or PayPal account; Dial reconciles their events and exports to QuickBooks Online, Xero or Pantry. Sales-tax filing and remittance stay with the host's accountant or tax service.
- **CRM and marketing.** Client records are a scheduling history. Deals, pipelines and sales activity live in Orbit, HubSpot, Salesforce or Pipedrive; newsletters, campaigns and marketing SMS live in Mango or the buyer's marketing tool. Dial's own messages are transactional or follow-ups the invitee was told about.
- **Clinical and regulated scheduling.** Dial does not claim HIPAA compliance or sign business associate agreements by default and is not an EHR. Clinics use Balm; other regulated buyers keep sensitive intake in their contracted system and use data-minimizing questions in Dial.
- **Studios, restaurants, shifts and field service.** Memberships and class operations (Strap), table reservations (Placemat), hourly shift scheduling (Lanyard) and technician dispatch (Glove) are separate products. Dial's group events, packages and round-robin serve general appointments and meetings.
- **Marketplace discovery.** Dial publishes the host's own pages; it runs no consumer marketplace, ratings or invitee-submitted reviews.
- **Automatic adoption of external edits.** Dial never silently adopts an arbitrary external calendar edit to a booking's time or location; the drift reconciler proposes and a person decides (`CAL-008`, `AGENT-004`).
- **Ranking without a model.** Slot recommendations stay deterministic (`AVAIL-007`); agents propose to people and never re-rank public slots.
- **US first.** Prices and payments are USD; SMS follows US TCPA, CTIA and A2P 10DLC rules; holidays are US federal holidays; invitee-facing languages are the seven of `LOCALE-001`, and the operator app is English. Other currencies, tax regimes and messaging rules come after.
